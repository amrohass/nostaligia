Reconciliation of six independent design reviews into one plan.
R1 = Copilot · R2 = DeepSeek · R3 = Replit · R4 = Gemini · R5 = ChatGPT · R6 = Claude

**Missing input:** the constraints block (hours/week, launch date, budget ceiling) was not attached.
§8 totals the effort but cannot check it against your calendar. See §10 Q1.

**A note on apparent consensus:** the review prompt dictated the output structure (verdict → findings

---

## 1. Reconciliation summary

All six converge on the two things that matter most and are almost certainly right about both:
**Supabase over Firebase** (6/6, and the reasoning is sound — spend cap, PostGIS, `pg_dump`
portability, and a read path that neutralises Firestore's SDK advantages), and **the client-side gate
is not a security boundary** (6/6). Beyond that, the set splits along a quality line rather than a
substantive one: R6 and R3 reasoned from the actual system — its politics, its lifespan, its
contributors' physical safety, its cost mechanics — while R1, R2, R4 and R5 largely ran a generic
BaaS-hardening checklist and pattern-matched the findings. That matters because **the single highest-
leverage finding in the entire set appears in only one review**: R6's observation that GitHub Pages
cannot set a Content-Security-Policy, cannot rate-limit, cannot mint a signed upload URL, and enforces
its bandwidth/build ceilings by turning your site off. You built a system whose top requirement is
abuse resistance and then put it on the one host with no enforcement surface. Four reviews never
mentioned hosting at all. **The single biggest change falling out of all six read together: move off
GitHub Pages to Cloudflare Pages + R2, and treat that as the precondition for half the other fixes** —
it is what makes CSP, rate limiting, signed uploads, Turnstile, and zero-egress media possible at once.
Two majority positions are wrong and I am overriding them (splitting the `posts` table; relocating
`admin.js` to a separate site), and the combined must-fix lists over-scope this project by roughly
double; §5 cuts accordingly.

## 7. Revised architecture

**Components**

- **Front end** — existing vanilla JS (`el`/`mount`, `I18N`, `DATA`→`store`, `UI`), History API routing, on **Cloudflare Pages**. `admin.js` in the same repo, dynamically imported on moderator login.
- **System of record** — **Supabase Postgres (EU/Frankfurt)**: `posts` (+`kind`), `media_assets`, `comments`, `likes`, `places`, `content_blocks`, `moderation_actions`, `audit_log`, `releases`. PostGIS on `places`/`posts`.
- **Media** — **Cloudflare R2**: `quarantine/` (private), `originals/` (restricted), `public/` (CDN-fronted derivatives).
- **Edge Functions** — `request-upload`, `submit-post`, `moderate`, `publish`, `takedown`.
- **Static releases** — R2 (or Pages) under `/v/{ISO-ts}/…` plus a root `manifest.json`.

**Read path (no database involved)**

Browser → Cloudflare CDN → `manifest.json` (TTL 30–60 s) → resolves the active release → fetches `feed/page-0001.json` (immutable, `max-age=31536000`) or `geo/{cell}.json` / `decade/{d}.json` / `clusters/z{n}.json` → media from R2 via CDN → per-item pages are prerendered HTML with OG tags, hydrated by the SPA after first paint. Client filters against a short-TTL `redactions.json`. **A public visitor generates zero database reads and zero metered egress.**

**Write path (every hop authenticated)**

Signed-in user → `request-upload` (verifies auth, daily quota, declared type/size; Turnstile) → signed URL → upload to `quarantine/` → processing function (magic-byte check, reject SVG, re-encode image / transcode audio → EXIF gone, derivatives generated) → derivative to `public/`, original to `originals/` → `posts` row inserted `status='pending'` under RLS → moderation queue in `admin.js` → moderator approves → trigger records approver + content hash + `audit_log` entry → row marked dirty for publish.

**Publish**

Debounced cron (2–5 min, single writer via advisory lock) → reads approved rows at a known DB revision → rebuilds only changed shards into `/v/{ts}/` → validates references, media availability, translations → writes the release row → **atomically flips the `manifest.json` pointer**. Rollback = flip the pointer back. Takedown does not wait for this: delete the object, purge the CDN path, add the ID to `redactions.json`.

**Where the trust boundary sits**

At **Postgres RLS + the Edge Functions**, nowhere else. The browser — including `admin.js` — is hostile input. Every mutation is denied by default and allowed only by an explicit policy with a `WITH CHECK`. Role lives in a claim set by a `SECURITY DEFINER` function, never in a user-writable column. The RLS test matrix (F19) is the proof, and it runs in CI.

**What runs where**

Cloudflare: static site, CDN, WAF/rate limits, Turnstile, media. Supabase: auth, database, RLS, Edge Functions, cron. Your machine / CI: `gitleaks`, RLS tests, weekly `pg_dump` + media sync to storage you control.

## Appendix — What all six missed

Shared blind spots. These are the findings nobody will catch for you, and I rate the first as more dangerous than anything in §4.

**1. The primary failure mode of this project is not a breach — it's an empty archive.** All six designed elaborate moderation pipelines. None asked how many items exist on day one, who is sourcing them, or what a visitor sees when the map has forty pins. Community heritage platforms die stale far more often than they die hacked, and your own earlier plan was explicit that this launches with a **seeded archive, never an empty map**. That requirement vanished from all six reviews and from the brief they were given. It belongs back in the plan as a milestone with a number and an owner (Q5).

**2. Editorial governance is a design requirement and nobody treated it as one.** Six reviewers modelled moderation as a _technical_ queue. But the hard questions in a memory archive are editorial: who adjudicates two contradictory memories of the same street, what happens when a family disputes a caption, who decides what belongs. That's your advisory circle, and it needs a documented policy before the first contested submission — not after. It's also the thing most likely to sink the project socially, which no amount of RLS prevents.

**3. Copyright provenance of _archival_ material.** R6 covered consent for people depicted and per-item licensing. But the harder question is unaddressed: who holds rights to a 1965 photograph of Al-Manara scanned from a family album, or one that originated in a newspaper or an institutional collection? A contributor granting you a license they don't hold is the standard way heritage archives acquire takedown liability. Capture the provenance chain at upload — "where did this come from?" as a required field — not just the consent.

**4. Voice notes are a signature feature and were designed by nobody.** They appear in your seed data as first-class content. Across six reviews they got one passing mention of transcripts. Unaddressed: they aren't searchable without transcription, they're inaccessible without transcripts, they're the most identifying medium you host (a voice is biometric — a real concern given the privacy threat model in F16), and Arabic ASR quality is a genuine open question. This needs a design decision before v1, not after.

**5. Nobody costed _your_ time — only money.** Six cost models, all in dollars, none in maintainer-hours. For a solo builder the binding constraint is your attention, and the ongoing load after launch — moderating, answering removal requests, checking the publish didn't break — is invisible in every review and every budget table here.

**6. All six reviewed a description, not the code.** Only R3 and R6 said so. There is a real, working front end with `tokens.css`, `i18n.js`, `admin.js`, seed data, and a live auth gate — none of which any reviewer read. Several findings marked `[assumed]` may already be fixed, and there may be defects in the actual `public.js` state handling that no review could have caught. Before executing M3, spend half a day diffing this plan against the real code.
